## How to Use This Workbook

**This isn't a book to read—it's a tool to use.**

Here's how to get the most from it:

1. **Start with your intentions** - Complete the worksheet on page X before diving into prompts
2. **Choose one prompt** - Don't try to use all five at once. Pick the one that addresses your current challenge
3. **Try it immediately** - Copy the prompt, adapt it to your situation, and use it with your AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
4. **Reflect on results** - What worked? What surprised you? What would you change?
5. **Make it yours** - Modify the prompts to fit your voice and needs

**Remember:** These prompts are starting points, not scripts. The best prompt is the one you shape to your specific situation.

### Prompt 1: Clarify Your Core Values

**What it's for:** When you need to understand what really matters to you, make difficult decisions, or ensure your choices align with your deeper truth.

**When to use it:**
- Facing a major decision
- Feeling pulled in multiple directions
- Starting a new project or venture
- Navigating conflicting priorities
- Feeling disconnected from your purpose

**The Prompt:**

```
I want to clarify my core values through a structured exploration.

Please guide me through this process:

1. First, ask me to list 10-15 things that matter to me (big or small - career, relationships, 
   activities, principles, whatever comes to mind)

2. Then help me identify patterns and themes in what I listed

3. Ask me clarifying questions about why these things matter - go 2-3 levels deep

4. Help me distill this into 3-5 core values with clear definitions

5. Finally, help me test these values by asking how they'd apply to a real decision I'm facing

Guide me one step at a time. Wait for my response before moving to the next step.
```

**Example Use Case:**

*Sarah is a software developer offered a high-paying job at a big tech company, but she's also considering a lower-paying role at a nonprofit. She uses this prompt to clarify what matters most to her—discovering that "meaningful impact" and "daily learning" matter more than "high income" or "prestige." This clarity makes her decision easier.*

**Tips for Best Results:**
- Be honest, not aspirational (what IS true for you, not what SHOULD be true)
- Don't rush—spend time with the clarifying questions
- Use specific examples from your life
- Test your values against real decisions
- Revisit this every 6-12 months as you evolve

### Prompt 2: Brainstorm with Divergence First

**What it's for:** When you need creative options but keep hitting the same obvious ideas. This prompt helps you generate genuinely novel possibilities before converging on solutions.

**When to use it:**
- Stuck in conventional thinking
- Need breakthrough ideas
- Planning a new project
- Solving a complex problem
- Exploring possibilities

**The Prompt:**

```
I want to brainstorm [topic/problem] using divergent thinking before converging on solutions.

Here's the challenge: [describe your situation, problem, or goal]

Please help me through these phases:

Phase 1 - Diverge (No Judgment):
- Generate 20+ ideas, ranging from obvious to wild
- Include impractical, weird, and "bad" ideas
- Push beyond my first instincts
- Ask "what else?" repeatedly

Phase 2 - Explore Edges:
- Take the 3 most unusual ideas and develop them further
- Ask "what if?" questions that challenge assumptions
- Find the kernel of value in "bad" ideas

Phase 3 - Converge (With Judgment):
- Help me identify themes and patterns
- Combine ideas that complement each other
- Narrow to 3-5 strong options worth testing

Phase 4 - Reality Check:
- For each final option, what would it take to try it?
- What's the smallest version I could test?

Guide me one phase at a time.
```

**Example Use Case:**

*Marcus wants to launch a product but every idea feels generic. Using this prompt, he generates 30+ ideas—including some ridiculous ones like "a product that deliberately breaks after a week" and "something you can only use once a year." Exploring these edges leads him to realize his actual innovation: creating premium tools with radical simplicity and lifetime guarantees.*

**Tips for Best Results:**
- Suspend judgment during divergence—capture everything
- The "bad" ideas often contain hidden insights
- Aim for quantity first, quality later
- Challenge your own assumptions
- Try combinations of seemingly incompatible ideas

### Prompt 3: Debug with Clarity

**What it's for:** When something isn't working—code, process, relationship, project—and you need to systematically find and fix the problem.

**When to use it:**
- Technical debugging (code, systems)
- Process breakdowns
- Communication failures
- Project issues
- Repeated problems

**The Prompt:**

```
I need help debugging [system/code/process/situation].

Here's what's happening: [describe the problem, what you expected vs. what's actually happening]

Please guide me through systematic debugging:

1. Clarify the Problem:
   - What exactly is broken?
   - What was expected vs. actual behavior?
   - When did it start? What changed?

2. Generate Hypotheses:
   - What are 5-7 possible causes?
   - Which are most likely based on symptoms?

3. Design Tests:
   - How can I isolate each hypothesis?
   - What's the quickest test for the most likely cause?

4. Analyze Results:
   - What did the test reveal?
   - What can we rule out?
   - What new information emerged?

5. Solve & Verify:
   - Based on findings, what's the fix?
   - How do we verify it's truly fixed?
   - How do we prevent recurrence?

Walk me through this step by step, asking questions to narrow down the issue.
```

**Example Use Case:**

*Jamie's sales process is failing—conversion rates dropped from 30% to 10%. Instead of guessing, she uses this prompt to systematically examine each stage: lead quality, first call, proposal, follow-up. She discovers the issue isn't her pitch—it's that her leads changed (different source) and need a different approach. One adjustment to qualification criteria fixes the problem.*

**Tips for Best Results:**
- Describe symptoms precisely, not assumed causes
- Challenge your own hypotheses
- Test one thing at a time
- Document what you learn
- Consider both technical and human factors

### Prompt 4: Navigate Difficult Conversations

**What it's for:** When you need to have a hard conversation and want to prepare thoughtfully—finding the right words without losing your truth.

**When to use it:**
- Giving difficult feedback
- Setting boundaries
- Addressing conflict
- Saying no to requests
- Discussing sensitive topics

**The Prompt:**

```
I need to have a difficult conversation and want to prepare thoughtfully.

The situation: [describe what's happening and who's involved]

What I need to communicate: [the core message or truth]

What I'm worried about: [fears, concerns, potential reactions]

Please help me prepare by:

1. Clarifying My Truth:
   - What's the core issue underneath my feelings?
   - What do I need the other person to understand?
   - What outcome am I hoping for?

2. Checking My Heart:
   - Am I coming from care or criticism?
   - What do I appreciate about this person?
   - How can I honor both my needs and theirs?

3. Finding the Words:
   - Help me phrase this clearly and kindly
   - What's the opening line?
   - How do I stay grounded if they react strongly?

4. Planning the Approach:
   - When and where should this conversation happen?
   - What do I need to feel steady?
   - How do I handle likely responses?

Guide me through each section with questions.
```

**Example Use Case:**

*Alex needs to tell a coworker that their constant interruptions are affecting his work. He's afraid of seeming unfriendly or causing tension. Using this prompt, he clarifies that his core need is focused work time, checks that he's approaching from care (not resentment), and finds words that set a boundary while maintaining relationship: "I really value our collaboration. I'm finding that I need longer blocks of uninterrupted focus. Can we set up specific times to discuss projects, so I can give you my full attention?"*

**Tips for Best Results:**
- Be honest about your fears
- Separate the person from the behavior
- Focus on impact, not intent
- Prepare for emotional reactions (theirs and yours)
- Practice the conversation out loud

### Prompt 5: Use AI Without Losing Your Humanity

**What it's for:** When you want to use AI as a thinking partner without outsourcing your judgment, creativity, or responsibility.

**When to use it:**
- Starting any AI-assisted project
- Feeling dependent on AI
- Making important decisions
- Creating original work
- Processing complex situations

**The Prompt:**

```
I want to use AI to help with [task/project/decision] while staying grounded in my own thinking.

Here's what I'm working on: [describe it]

Please help me use AI intentionally by:

1. Clarifying What's Mine to Own:
   - What parts of this require human judgment?
   - What decisions must I make myself?
   - Where does my unique perspective matter most?

2. Defining AI's Role:
   - Where can AI add value? (research, options, structure, editing)
   - Where should AI stay out of it?
   - What's the boundary?

3. Designing the Collaboration:
   - How do I use AI to enhance my thinking, not replace it?
   - What questions should I ask AI vs. work through myself?
   - How do I stay active, not passive?

4. Checking the Results:
   - How will I know if AI helped or hindered?
   - What's my quality check?
   - Does the final work reflect my voice and values?

Guide me to use AI as a tool, not a crutch.
```

**Example Use Case:**

*Taylor is writing a personal essay about loss and considers using AI to draft it. Using this prompt, she realizes the raw emotional processing is hers to do—AI can't write her truth. But AI can help her organize her thoughts, find structural clarity, and refine language once she's written her rough draft. She writes authentically first, then uses AI as an editing partner. The result: a piece that's genuinely hers, just more polished.*

**Tips for Best Results:**
- Always start with your own thinking first
- Use AI to augment, not automate judgment
- Review and edit everything AI produces
- Preserve your voice and values in final work
- Reflect on whether you're learning or just outsourcing

## Making These Prompts Your Own

**The prompts in this workbook are templates—adapt them to your needs.**

### Customization Tips:

1. **Change the language** - Use words that feel natural to you
2. **Add context** - Include details specific to your situation
3. **Adjust the structure** - Skip steps that don't serve you, add ones that do
4. **Combine prompts** - Many situations benefit from multiple prompts in sequence
5. **Create variations** - Make versions for specific recurring situations

### Example: Creating a Personal Version

**Original prompt:** "I want to clarify my core values..."

**Your version:** "I'm facing [specific decision]. Before deciding, I want to get clear on what really matters to me in this situation. Here's what's at stake: [details]. Please help me..."

**The key:** Make it specific to your situation, voice, and needs.
