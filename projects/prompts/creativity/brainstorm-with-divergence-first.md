# Brainstorm With Divergence First

**Category:** creativity
**Difficulty:** beginner
**Models:** GPT-4, Claude 3+, Llama 2+
**Use Case:** When you need to generate lots of ideas without judgment, then narrow down to the best ones

## Prompt

You are a creative brainstorming partner. Your role is to help generate many possibilities, then help evaluate them thoughtfully.

**Phase 1: Divergence (Generate Widely)**

For this topic: [describe what you need ideas for]

Generate 20+ ideas without filtering. Include:
- Some obvious ones
- Some unexpected ones
- Some wild/unconventional ones
- Some combinations of existing ideas
- Some that seem impossible (sometimes those spark the best real ideas)

For each idea, give it a catchy name and 1-sentence description. Don't evaluate yet—just generate.

**Phase 2: Convergence (Evaluate Thoughtfully)**

Now that we have lots of ideas, let's evaluate:

1. Which ideas make you curious? (Curiosity often points to potential)
2. Which could you actually execute on?
3. Which solve the problem in a different way than expected?
4. Which combine multiple good ideas into something better?

Pick your top 3-5 ideas and develop them:
- Why does this idea have potential?
- What would make it work?
- What's the risk?
- Who would benefit?

## Example Input

"I need ideas for how to help people use AI tools more effectively"

## Example Output

**Divergence:**
1. AI Prompt Library - organized collection of tested prompts
2. Video tutorials showing AI workflow
3. Interactive workshops where people practice
4. Peer learning groups
5. Chrome extension that suggests prompts
6. Slack bot that helps with ideas
7. Podcast about AI discoveries
8. Community where people share results
... [15 more ideas]

**Convergence:**
- Most interesting: Peer learning groups + community where people share results (they could combine)
- Most executable: AI Prompt Library (doable now)
- Most unexpected: AI coach that learns your style and adapts prompts

## Tips

- Don't evaluate during divergence—it kills creativity
- Quantity breeds quality—aim for lots of ideas
- Combine ideas from different categories—often creates better ones
- "Bad" ideas often spark good ones—don't filter them out yet
- Trust your curiosity—it's a good signal
- Execute on something, learn, then iterate
