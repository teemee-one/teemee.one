# Building with AI: From Idea to Real Value

There's hype around AI, and there's actual value. The difference is in how you build.

This is a guide for developers, founders, and builders who want to create things with AI that people actually use and benefit from.

## The Trap

Right now, many AI projects follow this pattern:

1. "AI can do X" (true)
2. "Let's build something with AI" (reasonable)
3. [Build something because it's possible]
4. [People don't use it because it doesn't actually solve a problem]

The trap is building what's possible instead of building what's needed.

## The Alternative: Build for Real Problems

Start here instead:

### 1. Find a Real Problem

Not "AI can do this interesting thing."

Real problem: "People spend 3 hours a week searching through documentation" or "Teams can't align on what matters" or "People want to understand their data but don't know how."

**How to find this:**
- Talk to people doing the work
- Notice where they struggle
- Ask "What would make this easier?"
- Listen for frustration and repetition

### 2. Understand the Problem Deeply

Before you build, understand:
- What is the actual friction?
- How much does it cost (time, money, quality)?
- Who has the problem?
- What have they already tried?
- Why hasn't it been solved?

Write this down. Make it explicit. This is your North Star.

### 3. Design for the Human, Not the AI

Here's the critical move: design for the *person* using it, not for the capabilities of the AI.

Questions to ask:
- What decision or action is this helping them with?
- What do they need to understand?
- What judgment do they need to apply?
- Where should the human be in charge?
- Where should it be automatic?

The AI is one part of a system. The system is designed around the person.

### 4. Start Small, Test Reality

Build the smallest thing that tests whether this actually solves the problem.

Don't:
- Build for scale
- Optimize prematurely
- Add features you don't have proof people need
- Over-engineer

Do:
- Make it work for one person or team
- See if they actually use it
- Understand why or why not
- Iterate based on real usage

### 5. Measure What Matters

Most AI projects measure: "Is the output plausible?"

Better measurements: "Does this actually help people?"

Examples of what to measure:
- Time saved per user per week
- Quality of decisions made (before/after)
- Whether people use it repeatedly
- Whether they recommend it to others
- Whether their work improved
- Whether they understand more

If you're not measuring whether it actually helps, you're building blind.

## Design Principles for AI Products

When building with AI, keep these principles in mind:

### 1. Transparency

People should understand:
- What the AI is doing
- Why it's suggesting this
- What could be wrong
- What judgment they need to apply

Don't hide the AI. Explain it.

### 2. Agency

People should:
- Be able to override suggestions
- Understand options, not just get one answer
- Make the final decision
- Learn from what the AI did

AI serves humans, not the other way around.

### 3. Calibration

People need to know:
- When the AI is confident vs. uncertain
- Where it's likely to be wrong
- What context it's missing
- When to trust it vs. verify

Overconfidence from AI kills trust.

### 4. Iteration

Good AI tools support iteration:
- Easy to refine
- Accept feedback
- Get better
- Show multiple options

Not just "here's your answer, take it or leave it."

### 5. Depth Over Breadth

Better to do one thing deeply and well than many things poorly.

- Does it genuinely solve the problem?
- Is it delightful to use?
- Does it save time and thinking?
- Would you pay for it?

If yes, you have something real.

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Chasing Cutting-Edge Models

Using GPT-4 because it's the best doesn't make your product better. Using the right model for your problem does.

Sometimes that's a smaller model. Sometimes that's non-AI. Start with what solves the problem.

### 2. Optimizing Engagement Over Value

"Our users use this 30 minutes a day" isn't success if they'd prefer to use it 5 minutes because they're done faster.

Optimize for value, not engagement.

### 3. Building Without User Feedback

You can't know if it's actually useful without asking people who might use it.

Talk to users early, often, and genuinely listen to what they say.

### 4. Over-Promising

"AI will revolutionize X" is great marketing. It's also how you set expectations you can't meet.

Be honest about what your tool actually does and who it helps.

## The Real Work

The hardest part of building with AI isn't the AI part. It's:

- Understanding the problem deeply
- Designing something that actually helps
- Building the infrastructure around it
- Refining based on real usage
- Maintaining and improving it over time

The AI is the easier part.

## An Example

Let's say you're building a tool to help managers understand their team better.

❌ Bad approach: "AI will analyze everything and tell them what to do"
✓ Good approach: "This helps managers notice patterns in how their team works, so they can have better conversations and make better decisions"

The difference:
- First one is AI-focused
- Second one is human-focused
- First one is probably not useful
- Second one might actually help

## The Question That Matters

Before you build anything with AI, ask:

"If there was no AI, would someone pay for this solution? If yes, what's the AI part of it adding? Does it actually make it better?"

If the answer is "it just wouldn't be possible without AI," that's rarely a good sign. Usually it means you're solving a problem that didn't need solving.

If the answer is "it's already possible but this AI approach makes it faster/better/more accessible," you might have something.
