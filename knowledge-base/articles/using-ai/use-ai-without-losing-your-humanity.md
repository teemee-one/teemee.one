# How to Actually Use AI Tools Without Losing Your Humanity

There's a tension at the heart of using AI: these tools are incredibly powerful, but that power can seduce us into outsourcing our thinking.

This isn't a screed against AI. It's a guide for using it in ways that serve you—that amplify your thinking, extend your capabilities, and help you move toward your goals—without atrophying the very faculties that make you valuable.

## The Problem

Most people use AI in two problematic ways:

**1. Avoidance** - "Just give me the answer"
- You ask for a conclusion, an essay, a strategy
- You get something plausible back
- You use it without understanding it
- You outsource thinking

**2. Over-reliance** - "I'll check if it's right"
- Similar problem, slightly different attitude
- You still haven't done the thinking
- You've just shifted from creation to validation

Neither approach uses AI well. Both atrophy your skills and miss the actual value AI can provide.

## The Better Way: AI as Thinking Partner

The best use of AI is as a thinking partner—someone who helps you think more clearly, explore more possibilities, and move faster.

Here's what that looks like:

### 1. Start with Your Own Thinking

Before you ask AI anything:
- What do *you* think about this?
- What are you uncertain about?
- What have you already tried?

This isn't about perfectionism. It's about having skin in the game. You should arrive with your own perspective.

### 2. Use AI for Exploration, Not Answers

Instead of "write this article," try:
- "I want to explore the idea that [my idea]. What assumptions am I making? What would challenge this?"
- "I'm stuck between two approaches. Here's my thinking on each. What am I missing?"
- "Help me brainstorm 20 wild ideas for [problem]. I want unexpected possibilities."

Notice the difference? You're using AI to expand your thinking, not replace it.

### 3. Iterate, Don't Absorb

When AI gives you something:
- Read it with skepticism
- What's genuinely useful?
- What's wrong or incomplete?
- What did it miss?
- How would you improve it?

Then—crucially—use your judgment. Iterate with the AI. Refine it. Make it yours.

### 4. Preserve the Learning

The goal isn't speed, it's better thinking and better results.

If you use AI to get an answer without understanding it, you:
- Don't learn
- Can't explain it to others
- Can't adapt it to new situations
- Haven't actually solved the problem

Instead:
- Work through the problem
- Use AI to help you think through it
- Learn something
- Build capability

## Practical Examples

### Writing

❌ Bad: "Write an article about AI and authenticity"
✓ Good: "I want to argue that [my specific argument]. Here's my thinking. What's wrong with this? What examples would make it stronger?"

### Coding

❌ Bad: "Write a function to do X"
✓ Good: "I'm trying to solve X with Y approach. Here's my code. Why might this not work? What's a cleaner way to think about this?"

### Analysis

❌ Bad: "Analyze this data"
✓ Good: "Here's my data. I'm seeing [pattern]. What else should I look at? Where could I be wrong?"

### Planning

❌ Bad: "Create a marketing strategy"
✓ Good: "Here's what we know about our users and our market. Here's what I think our edge is. What am I missing? What would a strategy built on that edge look like?"

## When AI Is Actually Helpful

AI shines for:
- **Exploring possibilities** - "What could I do with this?"
- **Pushing back** - "What's wrong with my thinking?"
- **Finding blind spots** - "What am I not seeing?"
- **Iteration** - "How do I improve this?"
- **Learning** - "Help me understand why this works this way"
- **Perspective** - "How would someone with deep expertise think about this?"

It's terrible for:
- **Outsourcing thinking** - "Just decide for me"
- **Skipping the work** - "I don't want to understand this, just make it work"
- **Replacing judgment** - Using it without questioning whether it's right

## The Real Edge

Here's what's really happening when you use AI well:

You're not getting faster at doing the same things. You're getting the ability to do *more* thinking, explore *more* options, learn *faster*.

The edge isn't in using the tool. It's in thinking more clearly about what matters, understanding the problem deeply, and making better decisions.

AI helps you do that. But only if you stay in the game.

## A Different Question

Instead of "How can I use AI to work less?", ask:

"How can I use AI to think better, explore more possibilities, and move toward what matters faster?"

The first is a race to the bottom. The second is how you build real capability.

---

*AI is a tool for extending human thinking. Use it that way, and you'll not only get better results—you'll become better at thinking. Use it as a replacement for thinking, and you'll lose the one thing that makes you irreplaceable.*
