# AI and Authenticity: Preserving What Makes Us Human

There's a deep anxiety underneath a lot of AI conversations: Will we lose something essential in the process of outsourcing more to machines?

The answer is: maybe. But only if we're not intentional about it.

## What We Risk Losing

### 1. The Authenticity of Our Work

When a student uses AI to write their essay, they lose something:
- The struggle that builds thinking
- The discovery that comes from working through a problem
- The ownership of the result
- The learning that happens in the process

The essay might be better. But the student is worse off.

This applies everywhere. When we outsource our thinking to AI, we outsource the part of ourselves that grows through struggle.

### 2. Genuine Connection

When communication becomes AI-mediated or AI-generated, something shifts:
- Less vulnerability (the AI smooths over rough edges)
- Less surprise (everything is polished and predictable)
- Less truth (the AI defaults to safe and popular, not authentic)

Real connection requires authenticity. Authenticity sometimes means imperfection.

### 3. Unique Perspective

AI is trained on what already exists. It's fundamentally regressive—good at synthesizing what came before, not good at the genuinely new.

When everyone uses AI the same way, everything starts to look the same. The weird, original, distinctive voice gets smoothed away.

## What Authenticity Actually Means

Authenticity isn't about rejection of AI. It's about:

**Being genuinely yourself**
- Your real perspective, not the popular one
- Your actual thoughts, even if imperfect
- Your genuine voice, not the polished one

**Having real agency**
- You're making the choices, not outsourcing them
- You understand your decisions
- You're responsible for the outcomes

**Engaging genuinely with others**
- Real conversation, not performance
- Vulnerability where appropriate
- Real stakes and real care

## How to Preserve Authenticity While Using AI

### 1. Do Your Thinking First

Before you use AI:
- Form your actual opinion
- Do the work you're capable of
- Know what you think

Then use AI to refine, explore, and improve—not to replace.

### 2. Preserve the Struggle

The struggle is where growth happens. Some things shouldn't be optimized:
- Learning something new
- Creating something original
- Working through a hard problem
- Having a difficult conversation

Use AI to help, not to eliminate the difficulty.

### 3. Protect Your Voice

Your voice is yours. It's shaped by your experiences, your thinking, your perspective.

When you use AI-generated content, ask:
- Does this sound like me?
- Would I actually say this?
- Am I helping or hiding?
- What's lost if I use this instead of my own words?

### 4. Choose When to Use AI

Some things are better AI-assisted. Some things are better done yourself:

**Better AI-assisted:**
- Editing and refining (you write first, then polish)
- Exploring options (you decide, AI helps you see more)
- Technical work (formatting, structure, optimization)
- Learning (explaining things to help you understand)

**Better done yourself:**
- Expressing something important to you
- Making decisions that affect your life
- Learning through struggle
- Connecting with someone who matters
- Creating something original
- Defining what you believe

### 5. Stay in the Loop

If you use AI:
- Understand what it did
- Verify it's actually right
- Maintain judgment and agency
- Stay accountable

### 6. Talk About It

Be transparent about AI use:
- In your work
- In your communication
- With people who care
- In public if it matters

Pretending AI content is yours is the opposite of authenticity.

## The Real Opportunity

Here's what's actually possible:

AI could free us to be *more* authentic, not less.

If AI handles:
- Formatting emails
- Finding relevant information
- Generating options for us to choose from
- Handling routine tasks

Then we have more time and mental energy for:
- Real thinking
- Genuine conversation
- Meaningful work
- Authentic creation

The question is: will we actually use it that way?

## The Choice Point

This isn't predetermined. It's a choice we make every time we use an AI tool:

- Do I use this to think better, or to think less?
- Do I use this to express myself better, or to avoid being myself?
- Do I use this to learn more, or to skip learning?
- Do I use this to connect better, or to hide?

These are individual choices. And they add up to cultural ones.
